Background

Roots & Dedication

Saman was raised in San Bernardino, California, in a single-parent, low-income immigrant household.

Growing up during the 2008 financial crisis and watching the City of San Bernardino navigate municipal bankruptcy in 2012, Sam witnessed early on the tangible impact of resource disparity, capital access, and municipal policy on working families. That formative background instilled an abiding determination to understand how legal systems, capital allocation, and public policy can be leveraged to build resilient, flourishing communities.

At UCLA, Sam majored in Public Affairs at the Luskin School of Public Affairs, graduating as a Phi Beta Kappa Key into Public Service Scholar and leading the Bruin Ambassadors Program to broaden higher education access for low-income students across Los Angeles.

Following college, Sam deepened his cross-sector experience as a Coro Fellow in Public Affairs in Southern California, advising on small-business financing and affordable housing development before matriculating at Yale Law School.

“Had I graduated with six figures of debt, I would not be able to go back to my home region or spend my free time there for the foreseeable future. With the freedom of the Hurst Horizon Scholarship, I can pursue what matters most: returning home to catalyze economic development in San Bernardino.”

— Yale Law School Student Profile
